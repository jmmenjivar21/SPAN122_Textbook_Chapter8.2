Cápsula Cultural · Capítulo 8.2 · El Salvador

Fernando Llort — La Semilla de un Sueño

En 1972, un joven artista salvadoreño llamado Fernando Llort (1949–2018) tomó una decisión que cambiaría el rostro cultural de su país. Dejó la capital, San Salvador, y se mudó a La Palma, un pueblo pequeño en las montañas de Chalatenango. Allí, mientras caminaba por las calles, vio a un niño que frotaba una semilla de copinol contra el suelo. Cuando la semilla se abría, mostraba una superficie blanca enmarcada por un borde marrón. "Es un cuadro enmarcado," pensó. Esa semilla le inspiró una visión entera.

Con esa idea, Llort fundó el taller La Semilla de Dios. Allí les enseñó a los habitantes a dibujar, pintar y crear artesanías. Les dio herramientas, les compartió su estilo y, poco a poco, transformó al pueblo. Hoy La Palma tiene más de 130 talleres, y miles de familias viven del arte que Llort sembró en ellas.

Su estilo es inconfundible: figuras geométricas, contornos negros gruesos, colores intensos —turquesas, rojos, amarillos, cobaltos— y motivos de casitas, palomas, soles, montañas e iglesias. Es un arte naíf (ingenuo) con influencias del cubismo.

1949–2018 La Palma, Chalatenango Arte naíf / cubismo La Semilla de Dios +130 talleres Arte de esperanza
¿Sabías qué…? DATOS
1Llort's unmistakable style features bold black outlines, intense turquoise, red, yellow, and cobalt colors, with motifs of doves, suns, mountains, and village chapels.
2Though he lived through El Salvador's civil war, Llort deliberately avoided political commentary — he wanted to give his people an image of esperanza (hope).
3Today his aesthetic appears at the international airport, in museums, on ceramics, t-shirts, and murals — it is recognized as quintessentially Salvadoran.
4Llort taught his art style freely to the local community, enabling thousands of families to earn income through craft — he sembró (planted) art as a livelihood.

G1Pronombres de Objeto Directo e Indirecto

OD answers ¿qué? or ¿a quién? — the thing/person directly affected. OI answers ¿a quién? or ¿para quién? — the recipient of the action.

PersonPronombre ODPronombre OI

tútete

¡OJO! · Doble pronombre · le/les → se

When combining both pronouns, OI always comes first. When le or les comes before lo/la/los/las, it changes to se.
Llort regaló su estilo al pueblo. → Llort se lo regaló.

OD example: Su estética: la conocemos, la reconocemos. (¿Qué? → su estética)
OI example: Llort les dio herramientas a los habitantes. (¿A quiénes? → a los habitantes)
Double: Llort regaló su estilo al pueblo. → Llort se lo regaló. (le + lo → se lo)

G3Ser vs. Estar — Advanced Contrast with Emotions

Many adjectives change meaning entirely depending on which verb is used. Context is everything!

Adjective SER · identity / permanent trait ESTAR · condition / current state
aburrido/aEs aburrido. — He is boring (trait)Está aburrido. — He is bored (right now)
listo/aEs lista. — She is clever/smartEstá lista. — She is ready
malo/aEs malo. — He is bad/evilEstá malo. — He is sick/unwell
rico/aEs rica. — She is wealthyEstá rica. — It is delicious
muerto/aEs un hombre muerto. — classificationEstá muerto. — He is dead (state)
La regla de oro

Ser = identity, origin, permanent characteristics  ·  Estar = location, condition, current state, emotions

Final VideoLog Presentation · Chapter 8

Una historia basada en una imagen

Choose a painting, photograph, or illustration from the Spanish-speaking world. Tell the story behind the image — what happened before, during, and after the moment captured. Include information about the artist. Narrate directly to camera using all the grammar tools from Chapter 8.

✓ 3–4 minutes ✓ Speak to camera ✓ Imperfect + Preterite ✓ 2+ irregular verbs ✓ 2+ object pronouns ✓ Narrative markers
Recording Checklist GUÍA DE GRABACIÓN

Opening: Introduce the artist and briefly describe the image and what it depicts using ser + descriptive adjectives and relative clauses.

Scene-setting (imperfect): Describe what life was like, what people were doing, and what the setting looked like before the key moment.

Key events (preterite): Narrate 3–4 events using the preterite, including at least 2 irregular forms. Use narrative markers.

Feelings segment: Describe how the subject(s) felt using emotion vocabulary and the ser/estar contrast.

Object pronouns: Use at least 2 object pronouns in your story (DOP or IOP).

Closing reflection: ¿Qué significa para ti esta imagen? ¿Qué sentiste al verla?

Duration & delivery: 3–4 minutes total; speaking directly to camera (not reading).

Nota Cultural · México y el Surrealismo

Mexico and Surrealism · A homegrown avant-garde movement

Nota Cultural · Capítulo 8 · Mexico

México — Capital del Surrealismo

Mexico became an unexpected home for Surrealism in the mid-20th century — not as a European import but as a deeply homegrown movement. Together, its artists made Mexico City a center of international avant-garde art — a tradition that continues today in galleries from La Colonia Roma to Coyoacán.

These artists refused to separate the personal from the political, the mythological from the scientific, the indigenous from the European. Their work reflects Mexico's complex identity as a meeting point of cultures, histories, and realities.

Mid-20th century Mexico City La Colonia Roma Coyoacán International avant-garde
Frida Kahlo
México · 1907–1954

Often labeled a Surrealist — though she rejected the label — Kahlo painted intensely personal imagery rooted in Mexican folk art and her own physical experiences. Her self-portraits explore pain, identity, and the body with unflinching directness.

Remedios Varo
España / México · 1908–1963

Born in Spain but exiled to Mexico, Varo created dreamlike paintings blending science, spirituality, and feminism. Her meticulous technique and otherworldly settings — towers, laboratories, forests — set her apart as one of Surrealism's most original voices.

Leonora Carrington
Reino Unido / México · 1917–2011

British-born, Carrington spent most of her life in Mexico City, weaving Celtic mythology and Latin American culture into her Surrealist canvases. Her work is filled with animals, witches, and ritual — deeply mysterious and richly symbolic.

Reflexión · ¿Y tu Video Log? CONEXIÓN

Any of these three artists could be the basis for your Video Log 8 painting. Their works tell rich, layered stories — exactly what the assignment calls for.

Think about: What is happening in the painting? What happened before? What might happen next? How does the painter feel — and how do you feel as the viewer? Use all four grammar structures from Chapter 8 in your narration.
